Performance support should make the next training decision clearer. Start with the path that best describes you, then choose the amount of support you need.

## Choose the Amount of Support

### One-Time Assessment

*Essentials*

Best when you need a clear answer to a specific performance question.

- Focused testing, assessment, or analysis.
- A concise report with key findings.
- Clear priorities and recommended next steps.

### Ongoing Performance Support

*Applied*

Best when an athlete and coach want guidance across a training phase or season.

- Regular testing or monitoring.
- Progress reviews tied to performance goals.
- Training recommendations informed by meaningful changes.

### Team Performance System

*High-Performance*

Best when coaching teams or programs need a consistent system around their athletes.

- Standardized testing and monitoring.
- Coach-ready dashboards and reporting.
- KPIs, decision thresholds, and staff workflows aligned to the program.

## How It Works

1. **Discuss the challenge.** Define the athlete's goals, the coach's questions, available data, and practical constraints.
2. **Select the right approach.** Choose the smallest useful set of tests, metrics, and support.
3. **Apply the findings.** Receive clear interpretation and priorities that can be used in training.

Support can be delivered remotely, at the client's location, or—subject to seasonal availability—for individuals and small groups at my location.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can support be delivered remotely?

Yes. Existing testing data and systems can be reviewed remotely, including test selection, KPIs, interpretation, reporting, and planning recommendations.

### Do you work with individual athletes?

Yes. One-Time Assessment and Ongoing Performance Support can be used for individual testing, training guidance, progress monitoring, and return-to-performance decisions.

### Can you work with existing coaches and performance staff?

Yes. Support is designed to strengthen existing coaching workflows and shared decision-making, not replace current staff or processes.

### Do we need specialized equipment?

Not always. The approach is selected around the performance question, available equipment, athlete population, and practical constraints. Equipment can be provided for some on-site work as needed.

## Not Sure Which Option Fits?

Describe your goals, current challenge, and timeline. The first conversation can identify the most useful next step without committing you to a larger system than you need.
